A weekly snapshot, per person

Food is one of the unavoidable costs of everyday life. But how much does the average person in the United Kingdom actually spend on it? The government's Family Food survey gives us a clearer picture of food bought for home and meals eaten outside.

For the financial year ending March 2025, the survey estimates £43.50 per person each week for food and non-alcoholic drinks, after adjusting spending figures for general inflation. This includes both food consumed at home and food eaten out.

01 / THE BIG PICTURE

Most food spending still happens at home.

In 2025, around 77% of spending on food and non-alcoholic drinks went towards food and drink for consumption at home, compared with about 23% for eating out. The figures describe average spending per person, not the cost of a typical household's entire shopping basket.

02 / INTERACTIVE DATA

How has the weekly bill changed?

Between 2022 and 2025, spending on eating out increased from £8.07 to £9.87 per person per week. This represents an increase of approximately 22.3%, while spending on food consumed at home remained relatively stable, rising from £33.45 to £33.63. This suggests that eating out is taking a growing share of the British food budget.

03 / THE TAKEAWAY

Eating out is taking a larger share.

Between 2022 and 2025, weekly spending on food and non-alcoholic drinks eaten outside the home rose from £8.07 to £9.87 in real terms. Spending on food for home moved from £33.45 to £33.63 over the same period.

“The food bill is more than a price tag: it tells a story about where — and how — people eat.”

These figures show that although the majority of food spending still happens at home, eating out represents a growing share of the British food budget.

However, these numbers are national averages. Individual spending can vary depending on income, household size, location and lifestyle.

The figures have been adjusted for general inflation, making comparisons between years more meaningful. However, spending data alone cannot tell us whether people are consuming more food or changing the types of products they purchase.

DATA & METHODOLOGY

Where do the numbers come from?

Figures are from the UK Department for Environment, Food & Rural Affairs (Defra), Family Food FYE 2025, based on the Living Costs and Food Survey. About 5,000 households are surveyed each year. We use Table 1.2, which reports spending on food and non-alcoholic drinks in real terms. The financial year ending 2025 covers April 2024–March 2025. Values shown are per person, not per household.
